# Changelog

All notable changes to the OAC Slot Configuration Data specification, templates, examples, and validator are recorded here.

The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Version numbers are OCP OAC workstream draft revisions, not semantic versions; a minor increment may still be a breaking change, and each entry says so explicitly.

Only the current revision is carried in the working tree. Superseded revisions remain retrievable from git history at the commit noted in their entry.

---

## [0.9] — 2026-10-09 — Workstream review revision 2

In-place revision of the v0.9 document, applying the twenty-four review items raised in the OCP OAC workstream review of 2026-10-08. The version identifier is unchanged. Nothing here changes a wire value: the two retired rejection codes are reserved rather than reused, and no identifier, `FileName`, or PDR field is renumbered.

**Breaking against workstream revision 1.** A Definition or Assignment composed against the previous revision may no longer validate — the `string` type and the `max_length` key are withdrawn, `metadata.iana_pen` is withdrawn, and `ocp.initial_power_level_set_w` is replaced by two fields.

### Removed

- **Section 5.2, "Why the Definition Is a Separate Document."** The section argued the two-document model against a strict-selection alternative that no reader of a published specification has in mind. It was a remnant of earlier drafting. Its one durable paragraph — that the OAC frames its own configurable surface — is now a sentence in Section 5.1.
- **The content-allocation test.** The former "does the platform need this value to decide whether to power the OAC at all?" rule placed content by elimination, which answers a drafting question rather than an implementer's. Section 5.2 is now titled *Relationship to the CID* and leads with the OAC Base Specification's own statement of the split — the CID describes the OAC to the platform, SCD describes the platform environment to the OAC — then expands the SCD half: 5.2.1 *What SCD Carries*, organized as platform identity the OAC cannot infer, the conditions the slot provides, and the operational configuration the platform selects; 5.2.2 *What Remains in the CID*; 5.2.3 *Vendor Extension*.
- **Section 10.5, "Reserved Identifiers."** The list of identifiers withdrawn during drafting described the specification's own history rather than any OAC's behavior. Section 7.4 already reserves the `ocp.` prefix for fields Section 10 defines, which makes every withdrawn identifier undefined without enumerating them. The former Section 10.6 renumbers to 10.5; `ocp.link_subdivision` cross-references move with it.
- **The `string` type and the `max_length` key.** A value an OAC cannot validate is a value it cannot act on, and text that labels rather than configures already has `display_name` and `description` to live in. Section 7.5 now reads "There are three types": `integer`, `boolean`, and `enumeration`. Rejection code `0x08 LENGTH_EXCEEDED` is retired to Reserved. The Section 7.4 limit of 128 characters on an *identifier* is unaffected and is now reported as `0x04`.
- **`metadata.iana_pen`.** Informational provenance that no OAC and no controller acted on. Vendor identity already appears where it matters, in the `pen.<IANA PEN>.` prefix of every vendor field. Tables 7.2-1 and 9.2-1 now close their key sets: an OAC shall declare no `metadata` key the table does not define, and vendor metadata belongs in a `pen.`-prefixed top-level key.
- **Annex D, "Items Open for Workstream Resolution."** Collecting open questions at the back of the document separated each from the text it bore on. The six open items are now **Work in progress** notes placed at the section they belong to: Section 9.4 (sparseness and the `required` vocabulary), Section 10.4 (the PLDM power effecters), Section 11.4 (the configuration-identity gap), Section 11.5 (how a rejection code reaches the controller), Section 11.6 (the whole latching model), and Annex C (the unfixed `FileCapabilities` bits).
- **The Foreword's revision-history note.** This is the first public posting of the document; a pointer to porting notes from an unpublished predecessor has no audience.

### Changed

- **Power clamping is replaced by a cross-field rule, and the set point becomes two fields.** The former Section 10.3 defined `applied_cap = min(ocp.initial_power_level_set_w, ocp.max_power_w, opl_max_w)` and forbade rejection on a contradictory pair. A controller that composes a set point above the budget it declared in the same document has made an error, and absorbing it silently leaves that controller believing something false. `ocp.initial_power_level_set_w` is retired in favour of **`ocp.initial_power_max_w`** (Section 10.3, response class `required`) and **`ocp.initial_power_min_w`** (Section 10.4, `optional`, defaulting to the CID `opl_set_min_w`). Both seed the OAC's PLDM power minimum and maximum effecters at entry to Operational State O5 and remain modifiable at runtime. Two relations are now enforced and rejected with `0x10 CONSTRAINT_CONFLICT`: the maximum shall not exceed `ocp.max_power_w`, and the minimum shall not exceed the maximum. Where a field was omitted from the Assignment, the comparison is against the value it defaulted to. Section 11.3.1 gains check 8, *Cross-field feasibility*.
- **A new Section 12, Runtime Power Control.** SCD is one-time configuration per negotiation; everything dynamic goes through the PLDM effecter path. The section states what the SCD values mean once the OAC is running: they are the effecters' starting points, not a ceiling on the effecters' existence, and an OAC shall not permit the power maximum effecter to be raised above `ocp.max_power_w` — it answers an attempt with an error rather than silently clamping, so a controller is never left believing it holds more budget than it does. Former Sections 12 and 13 renumber to 13 and 14.
- **`ocp.max_power_w` now defaults to the CID `opl_max_w`** rather than `opl_emg_w`. A default at the emergency level would throttle every OAC in every conforming slot unless the controller spoke up, which made the field mandatory in all but name and produced the Annex A anomaly of an initial set point above the budget. The obligation moves to the platform instead: a slot that cannot supply `opl_max_w` shall assign the field.
- **`definition_revision` becomes optional and informational in both documents.** Section 6.4 previously made it the sole staleness check, mandatory in the Definition, mandatory to echo in the Assignment, and backed by rejection code `0x0D`. Currency is now established by the re-negotiation model of Section 6.1: a firmware update that changes Definition content resets the OAC's management controller, `OAC_MC_RDY` deasserts, and the exchange begins again from retrieval. A signal edge carries the information that a counter in non-volatile storage was being asked to carry. An OAC shall not reject an Assignment over the value echoed in it, over its absence, or over its presence where the Definition declares none. Code `0x0D` is retired to Reserved. Section 6.2's two paragraphs on generated Definitions and stale stored copies are rewritten around the same model.
- **Sections 11.4 and 11.5 swap.** Configuration State Reporting is now Section 11.4 and Rejection Reporting Section 11.5, because a rejection code is only worth reading once the Configuration State Sensor has said there was a rejection. Table 11.4-1 is now State Set 15 applied to SCD; Table 11.5-1 is the rejection code table.
- **Invalid Configuration is stated more sharply.** State 2 of Table 11.4-1 previously led with malformed and out-of-range documents. It now leads with the case the workstream agreed the state is for: an Assignment that is well formed and structurally conformant but that the OAC cannot operate under — a power budget below what the OAC needs is the worked example — with malformed and invalid-value documents reported in the same state.
- **Section 7.7 is rewritten around what `response` classifies.** "`required` is advisory, not a hard gate" was found confusing in review, and fairly: a field can be genuinely required for the OAC to operate while still not needing to appear in the Assignment, because every field carries a default. The section now states that **`response` classifies the value, not the document**. A `required` field always holds a value, either assigned or defaulted; what the class records is that the default is a fallback rather than a good answer. An OAC still shall not reject an Assignment solely because a `required` field is absent, and the omission is reported as State Set 15 state 4, Missing Configuration.
- **Section 6.3 works from Annex C rather than from prose.** The field-by-field narration of entity identification is replaced by a pointer to Tables C-2 through C-4, with only the three rules that are genuinely requirements — association by entity identification, one `ContainerID` per OAC, distinct `EntityInstanceNumber` per file — retained in the body. Section 8.1 receives the same treatment.
- **Section 5.1 states where SCD sits on the power path.** The previous text said SCD "is not the admission decision" and stopped, which understated it: the OAC shall accept an Assignment before the baseboard energizes the primary busbar and asserts `OAC_PWR_EN`, and an OAC that rejects the configuration it is offered is not powered. The CID settles electrical and mechanical compatibility; SCD settles whether the OAC consents to the slot conditions. Both shall succeed.
- **Section 9.3 distinguishes the empty Assignment from the typical one.** An empty `assignments` object remains valid and still means the controller accepts the declared defaults, but the text no longer presents it as an ordinary outcome — it is a narrow case, reached when a single-OAC platform is content with a sentinel slot identity and the OAC's own power rating.
- **Size limits are 64 KB for both documents, in Sections 13.1 and 13.2.** The former 32 KiB and 8 KiB are replaced: KB rather than KiB, headroom for growth the examples do not yet exercise, and the same figure for both documents because an Assignment composed against a Definition cannot exceed it. The Section 13.1 calibration NOTE is recomputed against the six-field Annex A Definition.
- **Table 7.3-1 keeps `id`, `description`, `sentinel_default`, and `applies_when`,** all of which were questioned in review. `id` is retained because it is the Assignment's key and follows namespace rules that `display_name` does not; `display_name` is a label with no naming discipline and cannot carry that role. The ordering conventions of Section 7.10 are likewise retained, for the consistency they give tooling.

### Fixed

- **Annex A no longer shows an initial power set point above the platform budget.** The anomaly followed from the `ocp.max_power_w` default; with that default corrected and the cross-field rule in place, A.2 declares six fields and A.3 assigns `ocp.initial_power_max_w` at 1800 W against a 2000 W budget. A.4 explains the relation and what rejection would follow from breaking it.

### Artifacts

- **Templates.** `OAC-SCDD-TEMPLATE.json` carries the two new power descriptors, the corrected budget default, the closed metadata key set, and the three-type vendor stub; `OAC-SCDA-TEMPLATE.json` assigns both new fields and instructs the author to reconcile them before transfer rather than relying on a clamp.
- **Examples.** All six SCD examples are revised. Example B's Assignment now exercises the cross-field power relation; Example A's description explains why a Definition with no settable power band declares neither new field.
- **Validator.** `tools/validate-scd.py` drops the `string` branch and the retired-identifier list, checks `ocp.` identifiers against a positive Section 10 registry, sweeps both metadata objects against their closed key sets, enforces both cross-field power relations over effective rather than assigned values, and measures both documents minified against the 64 KB limit. The `definition_revision` comparison is removed, with a comment recording that its absence is deliberate.
- **Self-test.** `tools/test-validate-scd.py` grows from 33 cases to 42. Two of them assert that something is *not* reported — cross-connector group-name reuse, and a differing `definition_revision` echo — so that a removed check cannot be quietly reintroduced.
- **READMEs.** All five SCD READMEs are updated for the size limits, the power model, the response-class wording, the optional revision, and the retired keys and type.

## [0.9] — 2026-10-06

**Breaking.** Not wire-compatible with 0.7. An implementation conformant to 0.7 is not conformant to 0.9. The single controller-composed SCD document becomes two documents travelling in opposite directions, and the Format Identifier UUID that identified the 0.7 document is retired without replacement.

At a glance:

| Aspect | 0.7 | 0.9 |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Number of documents | One | Two: Definition and Assignment |
| Direction | Controller to OAC only | Definition OAC to controller; Assignment controller to OAC |
| Root key | `oac_scd` | `oac_scdd` and `oac_scda` |
| Relationship to CID | Strict selection over CID content | Independent configuration document with two named bindings |
| Transport | DSP0242 | DSP0257 for the Definition; DSP0242 for the Assignment |
| File identification | Format Identifier `7d1c4e93-6a2f-4b58-9e07-c4a83f52b16d` | File Descriptor PDR `FileName`: `OAC_SCDD.json` and `OAC_SCDA.json`. The 0.7 UUID is retired, not recycled |

The 0.7 model required every configurable attribute to appear in the CID first, because SCD was defined as a selection over CID content. That mis-stated what SCD is for. SCD replaces hardware straps, and a strap does not select from a menu the OAC published — it tells the OAC something the OAC could not otherwise know. Forcing every such value through the CID inflated a compatibility artifact with configuration content, and it meant that adding a vendor-specific field required a change to the CID schema. Specification Section 5.2 gives the full reasoning.

### Added

- **SCD Definition (SCDD)**, published by the OAC as a FRU Data File accessed per DSP0257 and named `OAC_SCDD.json`. It declares every field the OAC will accept, with type, bounds, response class, and a default.
- **SCD Assignment (SCDA)**, written back by the controller as a sparse DSP0242 PLDM file keyed by field identifier and named `OAC_SCDA.json`.
- **Mandatory defaults.** Every field descriptor carries a `default`, with no exemption. This is what makes a sparse Assignment safe.
- **Sparse assignment.** The Assignment carries only the fields the controller wishes to change. Omission selects the declared default and is a complete, valid outcome rather than a partial one. Together with mandatory defaults and advisory `required`, this is what lets a generic controller and an unfamiliar OAC interoperate: an unrecognized field is omitted and its default still fully determines behavior.
- **Sentinel defaults** (`sentinel_default: true`), a default placed deliberately outside `[min, max]` to mean "not assigned." Introduced so `ocp.slot_id` can carry a mandatory default without inventing a plausible-looking wrong answer. A controller shall never assign the sentinel value.
- **Vendor namespacing** via `pen.<IANA PEN>.<field_name>`, requiring no OCP registration. `ocp.` is reserved for the registry in Section 10.
- **`ocp.max_power_w`**, which runs opposite to every other field: the platform describing its own budget to the OAC rather than the OAC describing itself.
- **`definition_revision`**, an opaque monotonic counter the Assignment must echo. A mismatch means the controller composed against a stale Definition and must re-read.
- **`applies_when`**, a single-field equality predicate gating whether a descriptor is relevant. Deliberately not an expression language.
- **Power clamping.** Conflicting power values clamp rather than reject: `applied_cap = min(ocp.initial_power_level_set_w, ocp.max_power_w, opl_max_w)`. There is no cross-field constraint, because no assignable combination is unsafe.
- **Per-connector group-name scoping.** Protocol group names are unique within a connector, not across the document; the binding key is the pair `(cid_connector, cid_group_name)`. Cross-connector reuse is conformant, and Section 14.2 makes accepting it a mandatory controller requirement.
- **Rejection code table** covering eighteen distinct rejection causes plus `0x00 ACCEPTED`, including duplicate-key and null-value rejection. ECMA-404 leaves duplicate-key resolution undefined, so first-wins and last-wins are both wrong answers.
- **A PLDM data model for both SCD files.** Each artifact is now described in the OAC's PDR repository by a File Descriptor PDR per DSP0248, accompanied by sensor PDRs associated to it by matching `EntityType`, `EntityInstanceNumber`, and `ContainerID`: a File Size Monitoring Sensor reporting current size in bytes per DSP0242, and a Device File State Sensor implementing DSP0249 State Set 68 (Device File). DSP0242 makes both sensors mandatory for any file described by a File Descriptor PDR, which this revision now states rather than assumes, and the Assignment carries a third, the Configuration State Sensor of Section 11.5. The `ContainerID` is one value per OAC, shared by every record; `EntityInstanceNumber` is what distinguishes the Definition's records from the Assignment's. The State Set 68 enumerations are mapped onto each file in Tables 6.3-1 and 8.1-1, which also declare the states that shall not be reported: nothing reports wrapping or truncation for either file, because an oversize Assignment is rejected with `0x0E` rather than silently shortened. The Definition is `FileClassification` `FRUDataFile` with `FileCapabilities` permitting no write or modification; the Assignment is `OtherFile` with `FileCapabilities` permitting write and modification. Prior revisions said the Definition was FRU data and the Assignment was a PLDM file without saying how either was to be *found*, leaving a controller to discover them by PDR order. Each file is now identified by the `FileName` field of its File Descriptor PDR, stated normatively in Sections 6.3 and 8.1 and made a compliance requirement on both sides. Annex C gains seven field/value tables covering the new records and is reorganized into C.1 Definition and C.2 Assignment; the existing Table C-2 renumbers to C-5. A new Annex D.6 records that the bit-level encoding of `FileCapabilities` against DSP0248 Table 108 is open for workstream confirmation. Three disambiguations are stated explicitly, because each is a plausible implementation error: the Device File State Sensor reports file state and is not the Configuration State Sensor of Section 11.5; `metadata.definition_revision` and not the sensor remains the authoritative staleness check; and acceptance is never inferable from a size or state reading. `FileCapabilities` on the Assignment advertises what the transfer mechanism permits and does not relax Section 8.1 — an OAC still need not serve a previously accepted Assignment back. DSP0249 is added to the normative references at Version 1.4.0, which is the revision the quoted state sets are taken from.
- **A conformance example.** The third reference example is recast from a multi-group walkthrough into a cross-connector name-reuse case: two connectors each declaring a group named `pcie_primary`. A validator enforcing document-wide name uniqueness rejects it, which is the specific failure it exists to catch. A negative-case self-test accompanies it, asserting that the reuse is *not* flagged.

### Changed

- **The revision is numbered 0.9, tracking the OCP Open Adapter Card Base Specification.** SCD revisions follow the base specification they are written against, so that an implementer reading a base specification revision knows without checking which SCD revision accompanies it. The normative reference moves from base specification v0.7 to v0.9 and OAC-CID-SPEC is cited at v0.9. `schema_version` and `oac_spec_revision` are `"0.9"` in both documents, the specification file and the nine reference examples carry 0.9 in their names, and the validator recognizes 0.9 alone — a document declaring any other `schema_version` is answered `UNSUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION` (`0x0C`). Statements about which CID revision first carried a feature continue to name that revision rather than the current one: the per-group `name` and the removal of `link_subdivision` were introduced in OAC-CID-SPEC v0.8, and a pre-v0.8 CID still cannot satisfy a `protocol_group` binding.
- **Link subdivision moved from the CID into the Definition**, as `ocp.link_subdivision.<connector lowercased>.<group name>`. Subdivision is invisible to the powering decision, so by the placement rule it does not belong in the CID. Both identifier segments are required: drop the connector segment and two reused group names collide, and identifiers are the Assignment's keys.
- **`binding` renamed `cid_binding`**, so a reader encountering it is told immediately which document it reaches into.
- **Response classes reduced from three to two.** `required` is now explicitly **advisory** — a controller that does not recognize a `required` field shall omit it and the OAC shall proceed on the default.
- **Compression became mandatory above a size threshold** rather than purely advisory. Size limits are 32 KiB for the Definition and 8 KiB for the Assignment, measured as transferred.
- **Subdivision mode cardinality is now unbounded.** The 8-modes-per-group limit applied to the CID's `link_subdivision` array and does not survive the move; the Definition's `values` array places no cardinality limit. The companion limit of 8 protocol groups per connector remains normative in the CID.
- **The abbreviation `UMC` is no longer used anywhere.** The upstream management controller is named in full, or called "the controller" where the referent is unambiguous, matching the OAC Base Specification and the CID specification, neither of which abbreviates it. The `UMC` row is gone from the Section 4 abbreviation table.
- **The card is called the OAC, not "the adapter."** Every occurrence across the specification, READMEs, templates, and examples now reads `OAC`, which is what the OAC Base Specification and the CID use for the thing in the slot. "Adapter" survives only inside the proper name *Open Adapter Card*. Compounds collapsed accordingly: "OAC adapter" and "OAC-compliant adapter" are now just "OAC", and Section 14.1 is titled "OAC Requirements". This is editorial — no requirement changed, and no identifier, key, or UUID was touched. Note that "the OAC's management controller" and "the upstream management controller" are different devices; the specification distinguishes them by name at every point where both are in play.
- **The strap-replacement rationale is argued rather than asserted.** Section 1, Section 5.1, and Section 5.2 previously stated that SCD replaces hardware strapping without saying what that buys. They now give the engineering argument: a strapped interface has its width committed at board layout and *n* inputs distinguish at most 2^*n* untyped positional states, so extending it is a pin, a pull network, a board revision, and re-qualification. SCD is bounded by the Section 13 transferred-size limit instead, so an added attribute costs a schema revision and a `definition_revision` increment, and the `pen.` namespace admits vendor attributes without registration or collision. README.md gains a "What this replaces" subsection making the same case. No requirement was added or changed.
- **Section 5.1 no longer implies every OAC carries sixteen PCIe lanes.** The motivating example read "its sixteen PCIe lanes may serve one host as a single x16 link or two hosts as a pair of independent x8 links," which states as a property of the OAC something this specification never requires — an OAC's connector population, protocol mix, and lane counts are declared in its CID and are not constrained to PCIe or to any particular width. The clause is now written generically over a subdividable protocol group, with the sixteen-lane PCIe case retained as an illustration.
- **Section 3.5 now says where the upstream management controller resides.** The definition read "the management processor on the host baseboard," which describes only one of the two topologies the OAC Base Specification permits. It now states that the term denotes a role rather than a location, and cites OCP OAC Base Specification Section 10.2.1: the OAC Management USB 2.0 interface may be routed to a management controller on the baseboard or carrier assembly, which aggregates one or more OACs, or directly to a system-level controller on the primary system board such as the platform BMC, which then provides the functions otherwise required of the upstream controller. Where a baseboard or carrier controller proxies to a system-level controller, the requirements attach to whichever device terminates the PLDM exchange with the OAC's management controller. Editorial — no requirement changed.
- **Section 11.5 now carries a standard Configuration State Sensor instead of a bespoke mechanism.** The section previously recommended that an OAC expose SCD state "as a PLDM state sensor or dedicated command" reporting a `unassigned`/`latched` flag, the definition revision in force, an Assignment digest, and optionally which `required` fields had defaulted. It now requires a state sensor implementing DSP0249 State Set 15 (Configuration State), associated with the Assignment file, and maps the four enumerations onto SCD in Table 11.5-1: Valid Configuration and Missing Configuration are the two states an OAC reporting `latched` per Section 11.6.5 may hold, Not Configured is `unassigned`, Invalid Configuration follows a rejection under Section 11.3 with nothing accepted since, and Missing Configuration reports the case an implementer is most likely to miss — an Assignment that was accepted, and is in force, but that omitted a field the Definition marked `required`, so the OAC is running on a default it did not ask for. That state is the reportable consequence of `required` being advisory, and nothing else surfaces it, since nothing was rejected. The mechanism is mandatory where it was recommended, which makes step 6 of Section 11.1 a defined poll rather than an open question and removes the last use of `ocp.config_apply_timeout_ms`. The Assignment digest is withdrawn: State Set 15 carries no reference to the document that produced the state, so a controller can learn that a valid configuration is in force but not that it is the one that controller composed. Annex D.2 is rewritten around that residual gap rather than around the timing question it previously posed, and Section 11.6.6 now caches the echoed `definition_revision` and a defaulted-`required` flag in place of the digest.

- **Definition retrieval is specified as DSP0257 FRU Data File access, and DSP0220 is dropped.** Section 6.1 previously described the Definition in terms of the DSP0220 FRU File Descriptor Table — a resident flag, a `0xFFFFFFFF` size field — which is not the method this specification uses to discover or retrieve it. It now states what DSP0257 provides: aggregated FRU data is identified and formatted in the context of a FRU Data File, and that context together with the format of DMTF-specified FRU data files is defined in FRU Data File. The Definition shall be accessed by that method and by no other, located through the File Descriptor PDR of Section 6.3. DSP0220 is removed from the normative references and from every section and README that cited it. One discovery mechanism now governs, and it is the same one the sensors and the PDR repository already assumed.
- **Sections 6.1 and 6.4 no longer borrow their mechanisms from OAC-CID-SPEC.** The Definition's file classification was stated "per OAC-CID-SPEC Section 6.5" and its compression flag encoding "using the same flag encoding defined in OAC-CID-SPEC Section 6.4." Neither rule belonged to the CID, and neither now appears: the classification is restated against DSP0257 above and the compression section is withdrawn entirely. The scope exclusion in Section 1 also drops its citation of a CID section number. An implementation of this specification is now complete without recourse to OAC-CID-SPEC, and no SCD requirement can be invalidated by a renumbering of that document — a live risk, since Section 2 cites OAC-CID-SPEC v0.9 while the borrowed section numbers were those of v0.7. References to the CID that remain are structural rather than borrowed: the CID's own content and the platform's powering decision are scope exclusions, `cid_binding` resolution in Section 11.2 genuinely requires the CID, and Section 3 adopts CID term definitions by reference rather than restating them.
- **The Definition is now a stored file rather than a generated one.** Section 6.1 previously classified the Definition as non-resident and dynamically generated, and leaned on that classification for an argument that a stale Definition was impossible. It now recommends that the Definition be held in persistent, non-volatile storage on the OAC, with on-demand generation permitted as the alternative. Storage makes staleness possible, so the argument moves to where it always belonged: Section 6.4 requires `definition_revision` to be incremented whenever Definition content changes, a firmware update included, and an OAC that updates firmware without updating a stored Definition and its revision is explicitly non-conformant. A Definition that *is* generated on demand shall be deterministic for a given firmware configuration, and an OAC shall not return differing content under one `definition_revision` — previously implied by Section 6.4 but never stated.
- **The Assignment's lifetime is stated as a bound, not only as a storage class.** Section 11.6.4 prohibited non-volatile retention of the SCD cache. Sections 8.1 and 11.6.4 now also give the positive rule: the Assignment and anything the OAC decodes from it are held in volatile storage only, and their lifetime is bounded by +12V Aux. The management controller's working copy is aux-powered and the shadow record sits in the primary power domain, which cannot be energized while aux is absent, so removing aux destroys both copies and the OAC comes up `unassigned`. An OAC shall not retain SCD state across removal of +12V Aux by any means. This is the property a controller may rely on, and it was previously only derivable from Table 11.6-2 rather than stated.
- **File identification moves from `OemFileClassificationName` to `FileName`.** Both File Descriptor PDRs previously carried a vendor-chosen non-zero `OemFileClassification` and an OEM classification name — `oac_scdd` and `oac_scda` — as the discriminator a controller matched on. That mechanism is replaced by the `FileName` field of the File Descriptor PDR, a `strASCII` string, set to `OAC_SCDD.json` for the Definition and `OAC_SCDA.json` for the Assignment. A controller matches on that name to locate the record; the entry below on `FileIdentifier` gives the mechanic by which the file is then opened. The `OemFileClassification` requirements are withdrawn from Sections 6.3 and 8.1, from Tables C-2 and C-6, and from the compliance tables on both sides. The JSON root keys `oac_scdd` and `oac_scda` are unaffected and unchanged.
- **The file sensor requirements are restated against DSP0242 Table 4, which keys them to file characteristic rather than to classification.** An intermediate draft of this revision asserted that DSP0242 makes both an associated file size sensor and a file state sensor mandatory for any file described by a File Descriptor PDR. Checked against DSP0242, that is true only of a Regular File or a SerialTxFIFO. For a Fixed Length File the Device File State Sensor is mandatory and the File Size Monitoring Sensor is only recommended, because a controller finding no size sensor may fall back to `FileMaximumSize`, which DSP0242 makes exact for a fixed-length file. Sections 6.3 and 8.1 now report the standard accurately and mark where this specification goes beyond it. The association rule, the one-`ContainerID`-per-OAC rule, and the distinct-`EntityInstanceNumber` rule are each given their own paragraph so that they can be read, implemented, and tested separately; DSP0248's independent requirement that every file within a `ContainerID` carry a unique `EntityInstanceNumber` is cited alongside.
- **The two SCD files are declared as DSP0242 file characteristics.** The Definition is a **Fixed Length File** — read and modify after creation, never appended to, modified only when a firmware update replaces it — so its Device File State Sensor is mandatory per DSP0242 and its File Size Monitoring Sensor is mandatory per this specification, which tightens DSP0242's *should* and gives the reason: the fallback costs a controller an extra PDR lookup and a correctness assumption at exactly the moment it is deciding whether its cached Definition is still good. Because DSP0242 equates a fixed-length file's size to `FileMaximumSize`, the OAC shall now set that field to the Definition's exact transferred size rather than to a padded upper bound, and update it whenever the Definition is replaced. The Assignment is a **Regular File**, since Section 9 permits a sparse document and two Assignments for the same OAC may differ in length; for a Regular File DSP0242 does make both sensors mandatory, so that claim stands where it is now made. `FileMaximumSize` remains a genuine upper bound on the Assignment. The Definition shall not report State Set 68 state 3 (File is at Maximum Size), which DSP0242 advises against for a fixed-length file and which distinguishes nothing on a file that is always at its maximum size.
- **`FileName` identifies; `FileIdentifier` opens.** The previous revision had `DfOpen` taking the `FileName`. DSP0242 requires the `FileIdentifier` — a `uint16` carried in the same File Descriptor PDR, unique relative to the PLDM Terminus ID — as the parameter to `DfOpen`, `DfGetFileAttribute`, and `DfSetFileAttribute`. Sections 6.3, 8.1, and 8.2 now state the two-step mechanic: a controller matches `FileName` to find the record, then opens with the `FileIdentifier` from that record. This specification fixes no `FileIdentifier` value and a controller shall not assume one.
- **The `FileCapabilities` write bits are now fixed rather than described.** Sections 6.3 and 8.1 previously stated the access each file's capabilities bitfield shall permit without naming bits. They now set them. The Definition carries `FcWritesPermitted` = 0, `ExWriteOpen` = 0, and `DataType` = 0. The Assignment carries `FcWritesPermitted` = 1, `ExWriteOpen` = 1, `ExReadOpen` = 0, `FileModify` = 1 for random rather than append-only updates, `DataType` = 0, and `Read Pushed` = 1 — the last three being the combination that obliges the OAC to support the write command, so they are what oblige it to accept the transfer at all. Bit names throughout are the DSP0248 ones. Annex D.6 narrows to the bits still unspecified and asks the workstream to check the names it adopts against the edition of DSP0242 current at the time, since the two specifications have not always named these bits identically.
- **Sensor PDR field values are specified where DSP0242 constrains them.** A File Size Monitoring Sensor for either file sets `BaseUnit` to Bytes and `UnitModifier` to zero; a Numeric instance sets `RateUnit` None, `Offset` zero, and `Resolution` 1.00, and a Compact Numeric instance sets `OccurrenceRate` to No Occurrence Rate. The Definition's sensor should carry no thresholds, which DSP0242 advises against on a fixed-length file. The Assignment's sensor should set `FatalHigh` equal to `FileMaximumSize` for overflow detection, and an attempt by a controller to raise that threshold above `FileMaximumSize` shall be answered `ERROR_INVALID_DATA`.
- **The SCD files are declared a flat set.** Both File Descriptor PDRs shall set `SuperiorDirectoryFileIdentifier` to `0xFFFF`, which DSP0248 defines as "not part of any hierarchy." An OAC that does group its files in a directory shall use the Device File Directory PDR and Entity Association Record that DSP0242 requires, and the one-`ContainerID` rule then names the directory rather than the OAC. Either arrangement conforms; what the specification requires is that the value be common to all SCD records, so a controller that has found one can find the rest.
- **Table 11.5-1 separates initialization from runtime.** Each State Set 15 state now carries two meanings: what it signifies during Operational State O3, before the Assignment latches, and what it signifies at runtime from O4 onward. The distinction matters most at state 2, where an OAC that already holds an accepted configuration shall *not* move to Invalid Configuration on a rejection, and at state 3, where a controller finding Not Configured at runtime shall re-negotiate. Invalid Configuration is also pinned down: its characteristic case is a document that was well formed — it parsed, and its structure conformed to Section 9 — but that carried values which are not valid against the Definition. The formatting was acceptable; the content was not. Not Configured is stated as the default state an OAC shall report from management controller reset until an Assignment has been accepted or rejected, and an OAC shall not report states 1, 2, or 4 before the first Assignment of the current configuration cycle.
- **The CID dependency is no longer provisional.** The two changes this revision requires of the CID — a `name` on every protocol group, and the removal of `link_subdivision` — are ratified in OAC-CID-SPEC v0.8 and carried in the CID repository. The "not yet ratified" caveats that stood in the specification preamble, the Section 2 note, Annex A, and the four READMEs are removed, and Section 2 now cites the CID reference plainly. Nothing normative changed: the binding rules of Section 11.2 always assumed these two features.
- **The upstream management controller is named in full in normative text.** Every `shall` sentence that places an obligation on the controller now uses the full term, as do the Direction cells of Table 5.1-1 and the Overview diagram in README.md. Explanatory prose still says "the controller" where the referent is unambiguous; expanding it everywhere made the normative sentences harder to read, not easier, which defeats the purpose.
- **The Assignment write path is bound to `DfWrite`, and DSP0242 is now cited undated.** An intermediate draft of this revision left Section 8.2 step 2 unbound, on the view that no DSP0242 command carried payload from a File Client to a File Host. That was wrong: a File Client is permitted to write, and the DSP0248 `FcWritesPermitted` capability — "writing / modifying this file is permitted by File Client" — names the File Client as the writer. Section 8.2 now names `DfWrite` and states the requirements that go with it: `DfOpen` sets the write-access and `DfOpenExclusive` bits of `DfOpenAttribute`, with a non-exclusive write open answered `INVALID_DF_ATTRIBUTE`, because a second writer interleaving with the first would produce a document neither composed; `DfOpenRegFIFO` is zero; the Assignment is always pushed, so the controller does not use `DfOpenPolledPushed` to select a direction. Writes run sequentially from offset zero with no seeking, one section per transfer part, each file offset computed from the byte count the previous command returned, and a write inside the negotiated heartbeat interval keeps the session alive without an interleaved `DfHeartbeat`. Section 11.1 step 4, Table C-5, the compliance tables, and four READMEs are updated to match. Section 2 now cites DSP0242 without a version, so the undated-reference convention already stated there selects the latest published edition.
- **A write session, not the file length, delimits the Assignment document.** Over-writing a file in place need not shorten it — the resulting length may be the greater of the previous and the new one — and Section 9 permits a sparse Assignment, so a re-negotiated Assignment may be shorter than the one it replaces. An OAC that parsed the file by its length would therefore read the tail of the previous Assignment appended to the new one — a well-formed-looking document carrying stale values. Section 8.2 now requires the OAC to take the document to be exactly the bytes written between `DfOpen` and `DfClose`, disregarding any residual content. This closes the hole without obliging the controller to zero the file first, which would have required `FcZeroLengthPermitted` and, through DSP0248's configuration rule, exclusive read access the Assignment does not otherwise need. A compliance row on the OAC side records the requirement.
- **The Operational Power Level menu stays in the CID, unchanged.** An earlier draft of 0.9 moved it into SCD, leaving the CID with a bare `power_min_w` / `power_max_w` envelope. That was reversed on 2026-09-25: the OPL wattages are what a platform weighs when deciding whether it can power the OAC, and that has to be answerable from the CID alone. `opl_max_w` is additionally a hardware safety bound, and a configuration document shall never be able to raise a hardware limit.

### Removed

- **The `informational` response class**, along with its `value` key and its never-assign prohibition.
- **Format Identifier UUIDs for both SCD files**, together with Sections 6.2 and 8.2 that defined them. Each file is located by the `FileName` of its File Descriptor PDR, so a format UUID identified nothing a controller needed and carried the standing cost of two placeholder values awaiting Release Candidate assignment. The 0.7 UUID `7d1c4e93-6a2f-4b58-9e07-c4a83f52b16d` is retired and shall not be used for either document; it is retired rather than recycled so that a 0.7 controller meeting a 0.9 OAC fails cleanly to recognize the format instead of parsing the payload as a 0.7 SCD and misinterpreting it.
- **Compression, in full.** Section 6.4 defined a flag encoding selecting uncompressed, MessagePack, or Gzip publication of the Definition, Section 13 required compression above a threshold and recommended against it below approximately 512 bytes, Annex Table C-10 restated the flag values, and two compliance rows required publication and decompression support. All of it is withdrawn. Section 13 is retitled "Size Limits", and the 32 KiB Definition and 8 KiB Assignment limits are now measured on the minified JSON as transferred, with no compression stage to account for. MessagePack and RFC 1952 are dropped from the normative references.
- **`ocp.config_apply_timeout_ms`.** It sized the controller's wait *after* writing the Assignment, which determines no assigned value. See Section 11.5 of the specification for what step 6 now polls, and Annex D.2 for the residual identity gap.
- **`ocp.power_level_set_granularity_w`**, removed with the `informational` class. An OAC may expose step size as a PLDM sensor.
- **`ocp.slot_label`.** Human-facing labelling is not configuration.
- **`ocp.initial_power_level`**, superseded by `ocp.initial_power_level_set_w`, which is a wattage rather than an OPL name.
- **`ocp.secure_boot_enforce`**, insufficiently specified for an OCP-defined field. A vendor requiring it shall define it in its own `pen.` namespace.

### CID dependencies

Revision 0.9 depends on two changes to the CID schema. Both are ratified in OAC-CID-SPEC v0.8 and carried in the CID repository; the binding rules of Section 11.2 cannot be satisfied by a pre-v0.8 CID.

1. **Add `name` to every protocol group.** Unique within its containing connector; not required to be unique across the document. This is what a `protocol_group` binding resolves against.
2. **Remove `link_subdivision` from the CID.** It becomes `ocp.link_subdivision.<connector>.<group_name>` in the Definition.

The CID power block requires no change.

Because group names are scoped per connector, a name declared inside a shared `connector_profile` is unique within each connector that references it. Profiles may therefore carry names directly, and *uniqueness* requires no connector-level override. Whether one is wanted for *distinguishability* — a vendor naming the same profiled group differently on two connectors — is a separate and still-open CID-side question; see Annex D.4 of the specification. The SCD binding format is unaffected either way. The cost of the change is republication of the profile files with names added. The example CIDs in `examples/` are v0.9 CIDs and carry both changes.

## [0.7] — 2026-09-11 (superseded)

Initial DMTF-style specification, superseded in full by 0.9. Retrievable at commit `d6028d9`.

### Added

- JSON encoding over PLDM for File Transfer (DSP0242), replacing the earlier PLDM Structured Attribute / PDR approach in which subdivision was packed as a nibble per protocol group in a `bitfield32` and power expressed in 10-watt increments.
- A single `oac_scd` document composed entirely by the upstream management controller from what it could deduce from the CID.
- Slot locator fields, literal-string link subdivision selection, and Operational Power Level selection with an OPL-SET wattage.
- Reason code table for rejection reporting.
